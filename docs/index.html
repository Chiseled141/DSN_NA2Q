Both algorithms are evaluated under an equal total environment interaction budget (5 million steps for Scenario 1), ensuring a fair comparison. NA²Q employs value decomposition with GRU-based per-agent networks and a GAM mixer for credit assignment, trained with epsilon-greedy exploration. HiT-MAC uses asynchronous A3C across 16 parallel workers with a two-phase hierarchical training procedure.

The dashed red line marks HiT-MAC's phase transition: Phase 1 trains the executor policy (sensor aiming via A3C); Phase 2 freezes the executor and trains the coordinator (Shapley-value credit assignment) using a scripted executor surrogate. The observed coverage increase at this boundary reflects the scripted executor's deterministic aiming — not an improvement in the learned coordinator policy.
