Training Note

NA²Q — Well Optimized

NA²Q has been trained for 70,000 episodes with optimized hyperparameters. The attention mechanism enables efficient credit assignment and demonstrates strong convergence in cooperative sensor coordination tasks.

HiT-MAC — Parameters Not Fine-Tuned

HiT-MAC was also trained for 70,000 episodes, but the results remain poor because the hyperparameters have not been fine-tuned. Default parameters were used throughout training, leading to suboptimal performance compared to NA²Q.

Note: HiT-MAC's poor performance is due to untuned hyperparameters, not the algorithm itself. A properly fine-tuned training run is needed for a fair comparison.

Coverage Rate 100-ep avg

What this shows: Coverage rate measures the percentage of targets simultaneously within at least one sensor's field-of-view at any given step. A higher value means more targets are being monitored at once.

NA²Q (green) quickly ramps from ~30% to a stable 55–65% coverage within the first 5,000 episodes and sustains it throughout training — indicating the attention-based credit assignment successfully teaches sensors to spread out and coordinate their viewing angles.

HiT-MAC (red) plateaus at a low ~20–25% and shows almost no improvement over 70,000 episodes. This is a direct consequence of untuned hyperparameters: the agents fail to learn cooperative coverage strategies and effectively behave near-randomly.

Why the gap matters: A ~35-percentage-point difference in stable coverage demonstrates that NA²Q's neighbourhood attention mechanism fundamentally enables better multi-agent coordination, not just incremental improvement.
Note: Coverage chart shows 100-episode moving average for clarity. HiT-MAC data is converted from steps to episodes (100 steps = 1 episode).
